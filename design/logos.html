omp-ui · identity

A mark for the window
that runs your agents.

Eight directions, each drawn as a single SVG that survives being shrunk to 16 pixels or blown up to 512. They lean on omp's own vocabulary — the magenta accent, the near-black ground, the terminal — without borrowing omp's glyph, so the app reads as its own thing.

Every mark below is drawn with the same rules: one accent colour doing the work, geometry that holds together at 16 px, and no detail that is not load-bearing. The light swatch on the right of each card shows the mark on a pale ground.

The caret

The prompt mark, with the cursor as the bar that follows it. Reads as “you type, it answers” in one shape, and as a piece of text at any size.

Best for: the in-app mark and the Dock icon. The most legible of the set at 16 px.

The pane

One window, two sides: you on the left, the agent on the right. The division is the app’s whole idea — many projects, one place to watch them.

Best for: the app icon. It has a natural square and a silhouette that survives a 16 px tile.

The stack

Projects, stacked, with one of them live. The plainest statement of the app’s job: several things at once, one of them in motion.

Best for: a wordmark lockup. A little anonymous on its own — it could be any list.

The bracket

Code, with something alive inside it. The dot is the agent: the one thing in the middle that is awake while the rest of the project is still.

Best for: the in-app rail, where it sits next to other code-shaped marks.

The node

The agent at the centre, its tools in orbit. It is the one mark here that draws the relationship rather than the object.

Best for: the app icon, if you want something that says agent rather than terminal.

The pulse

A live trace inside a window: the app while the agent is working. It has the energy of the omp.sh particle art without copying it.

Best for: the app icon. The busiest of the set — it wants to be at least 32 px.

The gate

Something arrives at the door of the project. The arrow is the request, the frame is the workspace the agent is let into.

Best for: a wordmark lockup — it is wide, and wants the name beside it.

The twin

A nod to omp’s own glyph, squared and redrawn so the app is clearly an omp thing without being a copy of the brand.

Best for: the in-app mark, beside the omp wordmark in the title bar. Pair it with the word, never alone.

As app icons

The three short-listed marks, drawn as the tile macOS actually shows — with the mark inset, a hairline edge, and the whole thing scaled down to the 16 px the Dock throws at it.

Where I would start

  • The caret — the most legible at every size, and the least clever. It works as a Dock icon, in the rail, and next to the wordmark.
  • The pane — the only one that shows the app’s actual idea (you on one side, the agent on the other) while still being a clean tile.
  • The twin — the safest, if the mark has to say “omp” at a glance. It needs the wordmark beside it.
